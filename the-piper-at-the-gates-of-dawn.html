About the album

The Piper at the Gates of Dawn is Pink Floyd's debut album and the only one made under the leadership of Syd Barrett. He wrote most of the songs, with lyrics that are often playful and childlike, from a gnome and a scarecrow to a cat called Lucifer Sam. The band added the group-written pieces "Pow R. Toc H." and "Interstellar Overdrive", while Roger Waters wrote "Take Up Thy Stethoscope and Walk".

The title comes from the seventh chapter of Kenneth Grahame's children's book The Wind in the Willows. By the time the album came out, the band had made a name at London's UFO Club and had two hit singles, "Arnold Layne" and "See Emily Play", which were not included on it.

It was recorded at EMI's studios in London (today's Abbey Road) with producer Norman Smith. While working there, the band sat in on a Beatles session for "Lovely Rita". The album reached number six in the UK and is now seen as a key psychedelic record. Rolling Stone placed it at number 253 in its 2020 list of the 500 greatest albums.

Song stories

How the songs came together in the studio. Producer Norman Smith pushed the band away from long live jams towards shorter songs, and most of these tracks were shaped by that tug of war.

Matilda Mother

The first song recorded for the album, on 21 February 1967, in six takes. It was still called "Matilda's Mother" at that point.

Interstellar Overdrive

The band had already recorded an early version in January 1967 for Peter Whitehead's film Tonite Let's All Make Love in London. The album version followed on 27 February, with another attempt in March to make a shorter cut. The studio engineer, Pete Bown, was stunned by how loud the band was when he first heard it. Norman Smith adds a drum roll to the track.

Chapter 24

Recorded on 27 February, the same day as the first takes of "Interstellar Overdrive". Barrett took its words from an ancient Chinese text.

Flaming

Barrett first called it "Snowing". It was recorded in a single take on 16 March with just one vocal overdub. In the US it became the album's only single, backed with "The Gnome", in November 1967.

The Gnome

Recorded in six takes on 19 March 1967.

Take Up Thy Stethoscope and Walk

Waters's only writing credit on the album. The band recorded it on 20 March, the day after "The Gnome".

The Scarecrow

Recorded in one take on 22 March. It reached listeners before the album, as the B-side of the "See Emily Play" single in June 1967.

Astronomy Domine, Interstellar Overdrive and Pow R. Toc H.

These three began life as long instrumentals and were reworked over several weeks, between 21 March and 12 April. The band recorded 14 takes of "Astronomy Domine" in one seven-hour session. Their manager Peter Jenner provides the spoken voices at the start of that song.

"Astronomy Domine" stayed with the band. It appears on the live disc of Ummagumma, returned on the 1994 Division Bell tour and the PULSE album, and Gilmour played it again on his own tours.

Lucifer Sam

It started life as "Percy the Rat Catcher", recorded in April 1967 and given overdubs over five sessions. It was mixed in late June and renamed along the way. Barrett brought it back with his short-lived band Stars in 1972.

Bike

One of the oldest songs on the album: Barrett wrote it in late 1966, before the sessions began. It was recorded on 21 May 1967 as "The Bike Song".

Remember a Day

Not on this album, but recorded during its sessions, on 9 May 1967. Richard Wright's song was held back and appeared on A Saucerful of Secrets the following year.
