About the album

Ummagumma is a double album: one disc of live recordings and one of studio work in which each band member got half an LP side to make a solo piece, with no help from the others. Richard Wright suggested the idea, as he wanted to make "real music", and the pieces run in the order Wright, Waters, Gilmour, Mason. It was the band's first record on the Harvest label.

The live disc features fan favourites the band planned to drop from their set. The sleeve says it was recorded in June 1969, but it actually comes from two shows on the Man and the Journey tour: Mothers Club in Birmingham on 27 April and Manchester College of Commerce on 2 May. The title is said to come from Cambridge slang for sex, used by the band's friend and sometime roadie Iain "Emo" Moore, though Moore claimed he made it up. Nick Mason said they picked it because it sounded interesting and nice.

It reached number five in the UK and number 74 in the US, the band's first appearance in the American top 100. Critics were kind at the time, but the band themselves have been dismissive over the years. Waters called it a disaster, Gilmour thought the live disc was acceptable but disliked the rest, and Mason called it a failed experiment that, importantly, they never repeated.

Song stories

The live disc has the old songs, and the studio disc is four solo experiments.

The live disc

Wright later said the recording of "A Saucerful of Secrets" is a mix of both shows. The band also recorded a show at Bromley Technical College on 26 April, which wasn't used, and a live "Interstellar Overdrive" meant for the live disc was left off. A studio recording of "Embryo" was made before the solo-pieces plan was decided, and it was left off too.

Sysyphus (Wright)

Named after the figure from Greek mythology, usually spelled Sisyphus. It combines piano, organ, Mellotron and percussion in four parts. Wright started out enthusiastic about his solo piece but later described it as pretentious.

Grantchester Meadows (Waters)

A gentle acoustic song about Waters's youth in the Cambridge area. It grew out of an instrumental the band played at times, which often opened their concerts in 1969, with the vocals added later.

Several Species of Small Furry Animals... (Waters)

A collage of voices and percussion played at different speeds, forwards and backwards, inspired by the experimental musician Ron Geesin, who later worked with both Waters and the band. The sound of a swatted fly links this track to "Grantchester Meadows".

The Narrow Way (Gilmour)

Gilmour was nervous about making a solo piece and said he went into the studio and pieced bits together. Part one had already been played as "Baby Blue Shuffle in D Major" on a BBC radio session in December 1968. He asked Waters to write lyrics, but Waters said no. Part three was played live briefly in early 1969.

The Grand Vizier's Garden Party (Mason)

Mason's three parts feature his wife Lindy on flute in the first and third parts, uncredited. The seven-minute middle part mixes percussion, tape effects and a drum solo. It was never played live, but a similar solo called "Doing It" was part of the live Man suite.

The cover

Hipgnosis made the front cover with a Droste effect, in which the same picture of the band repeats inside itself, with the band members swapping places in each copy. It was the last Pink Floyd cover to show the band. On the back, roadies Alan Styles and Peter Watts stand next to the band's equipment laid out on a taxiway at Biggin Hill Airport, an idea from Mason, copying the "exploded" drawings of military aircraft.
