About the album

A Saucerful of Secrets is Pink Floyd's second album and the one where the band changes shape. Syd Barrett's health got worse during the sessions, and David Gilmour was brought in as a fifth member in December 1967. One of his first jobs was to mime Barrett's guitar playing in a promotional film. By early 1968 Barrett was out, and the band finished the record without him.

Where the debut was mostly Barrett's songs, here every member writes and sings. Barrett has just one song, "Jugband Blues", and "Set the Controls for the Heart of the Sun" is the only track with all five men on it. Gilmour plays on all but two songs.

The cover was the first of many Pink Floyd sleeves by the design group Hipgnosis, created by Storm Thorgerson, and includes a figure of Doctor Strange taken from a Marvel comic. The album reached number nine in the UK. Nick Mason has called it his favourite Pink Floyd record, describing it as a gradual cross-fade between Barrett's era and Gilmour's, not a sudden cut.

Song stories

The album was pieced together over a year of upheaval, and several songs were written or reshaped around Barrett's exit.

Let There Be More Light

Waters built it from a bass riff that came out of "Interstellar Overdrive". A jam the band recorded on 18 January 1968, with Norman Smith joining in and Barrett absent, later became its intro. Barrett's manager said he played the closing slide solo, but that has never been confirmed. In the US it was the album's single, backed with "Remember a Day".

Remember a Day

Richard Wright's song was recorded on 9 May 1967 during the sessions for the first album and held back. Barrett added slide guitar to it that October.

Set the Controls for the Heart of the Sun

One of the first two songs recorded for the album, on 7 and 8 August 1967, and first played live with Barrett. Waters took its words from a book of Chinese poetry from the Tang dynasty. Waters's vocals and Wright's organ were added in January 1968, and it stayed in the live set until 1973.

Corporal Clegg

The first Pink Floyd song about war, a subject Waters kept returning to until The Final Cut. It was recorded in January 1968 without Barrett, with Nick Mason sharing lead vocals. It was announced as the next single in February, but under pressure from the record label it went on the album instead and "It Would Be So Nice" came out as the single in April.

A Saucerful of Secrets

It began as a new version of the band's jam "Nick's Boogie". With too little material to fill an album, the band built the piece up, and Mason and Waters sketched it out like an architect's plan, with peaks and quiet valleys. Norman Smith disliked the idea and wanted three-minute songs. It stayed in the live show until the summer of 1972.

See-Saw

Wright's song grew out of a track the band recorded on 24 and 25 January 1968 under the working title "The Most Boring Song I've Ever Heard Bar 2".

Jugband Blues

Barrett's last song with the band, recorded on 19 October 1967. He asked for a Salvation Army brass band to play on it, and told Smith to let them play whatever they liked, while Smith insisted on written parts. The lyrics are often read as a comment on Barrett's own departure. The management wanted it released as a single, but the band and Smith blocked it.

Left off: Scream Thy Last Scream and Vegetable Man

Two Barrett songs from the same sessions were planned as a single, but the record company vetoed it twice. Waters kept both off the album, and Peter Jenner said "Vegetable Man" was too dark. They were finally released officially in 2016 on the box set The Early Years 1965–1972.
