Meddle

Gear: Meddle

Gilmour experimented with guitars, effects and recording tricks more on this album than on any before it. This page is a short overview.

Guitars

The main guitar was the Black Strat, a 1969 Stratocaster with a black body and a maple neck, which Gilmour first used on stage in mid-1970. For the "Echoes" sessions he also used a custom Bill Lewis guitar with a mahogany body and 24 frets, and a Fender 1000 twin-neck pedal steel guitar was used for "One of These Days". On stage he still played the natural-finish Telecaster too. The acoustic parts use an ordinary steel-string guitar.

Amplifiers

The core of the rig was Hiwatt DR103 100-watt heads with WEM Super Starfinder 200 cabinets loaded with four 12-inch speakers. On stage he used three of the heads, one of them a spare. Late in 1971 he also began using a solid-state HH IC-100 amp, perhaps as a preamp for the rest of the rig, and a Leslie rotating speaker cabinet. The IC-100 was also used for the tremolo sound of the bass solo that opens "One of These Days".

Effects

A Dallas Arbiter Fuzz Face, a Vox wah-wah, a DeArmond volume pedal and a Binson Echorec delay. One of the biggest changes in his live sound this year was a switch to a silicon-transistor Fuzz Face, which sounds brighter and more aggressive than the germanium one he'd used before. Nobody knows for sure which one he used on the album. The famous backwards wah-wah effect on "Echoes" comes from plugging the guitar into the pedal's output, as explained on the album page.

The rest of the band

Richard Wright: the gear list on Richard Wright's official website (compiled by the fan 'aZiMuTh', and not verified in detail) names a Farfisa Combo Compact-Duo organ, a Hammond M-102 'Spinet' organ with a Leslie 145 cabinet, an acoustic grand piano with a Leslie 145 cabinet, and an EMS VCS3 ('Putney') synthesizer for this album. Wikipedia credits him with keyboards and vocals, and tells the story of the Leslie-treated piano ping that opens "Echoes".

Roger Waters played bass, plus acoustic guitar on two songs, and he and Gilmour doubled the bass line of "One of These Days" on two basses. Nick Mason played drums and percussion.

We haven't found a reliable source for the exact model of Waters's bass or Mason's drum kit, so they are left out for now.
