Atom Heart Mother

Gear: Atom Heart Mother

A year of big changes for Gilmour's equipment: his white Stratocaster was stolen, a guitar later famous as the Black Strat appeared, and he began collecting guitars. This page is a short overview.

Guitars

Gilmour started the sessions with his white 1960s Stratocaster, but it was stolen in May 1970, when the band's equipment was robbed during their US tour. In June, at the Bath Festival, he played the guitar that became known as the Black Strat for the first time. He also still used his natural-finish Telecaster, mainly for performing the title suite in 1970–71.

Around this time he began collecting guitars. On the band's autumn 1970 tour of the US and Canada he bought a blonde Gibson Les Paul TV and a custom guitar from the maker Bill Lewis, and the Lewis guitar was later heard on Meddle and The Dark Side of the Moon. The album's credits also list Gilmour on pedal steel guitar, acoustic and classical guitars.

Amplifiers

Hiwatt amplifier heads with WEM speaker cabinets, which had replaced his Selmers in the second half of 1969.

Effects

A Dallas Arbiter Fuzz Face, a wah-wah and a Binson Echorec delay, plus a Leslie speaker cabinet for the recording of "Alan's Psychedelic Breakfast". Gilmour rolled his guitar's volume down to get overdrive for rhythm parts.

The rest of the band

Richard Wright: the gear list on Richard Wright's official website (compiled by the fan 'aZiMuTh', and not verified in detail) names a Farfisa Combo Compact-Duo organ, a Hammond M-102 'Spinet' organ with a Leslie 145 cabinet, a Steinway & Sons baby grand piano and a Mellotron M400 Mark II for this album. Wikipedia's credits agree, with the Mellotron on the title suite.

Roger Waters played bass, plus acoustic guitar on "If". Nick Mason played drums and percussion. Brass, cello and choir were hired for the title suite and arranged by Ron Geesin.

We haven't found a reliable source for the exact model of Waters's bass or Mason's drum kit, so they are left out for now.
