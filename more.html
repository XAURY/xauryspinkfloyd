About the album

More is Pink Floyd's first soundtrack album and their third studio album. It was made for More, the first film by the director Barbet Schroeder, shot mostly on Ibiza. It is also the first album without any input from Syd Barrett, and the first the band produced themselves, without Norman Smith's help (he kept an executive producer credit). David Gilmour sings every lead vocal.

Schroeder was a fan and brought a rough cut of the film to London. He didn't want ordinary background music, so he asked for songs that could be heard inside the film, such as a record playing at a party. The band worked out most of the music in about two weeks, semi-improvised, and without a proper dubbing studio they simply timed the film's scenes with a stopwatch. Roger Waters wrote most of the lyrics in the breaks between recording backing tracks.

The sound ranges from gentle acoustic ballads to hard rock and experimental instrumentals. The sleeve is a still from the film of two people playing around a windmill, darkroom-processed by Hipgnosis to look like a psychedelic trip. Reviews at the time were mixed, but the album reached number nine in the UK. Several songs lived on in the band's live shows for years.

Song stories

Some of these songs were written to fit scenes in the film, and some are heard by the characters themselves.

Cirrus Minor and Green Is the Colour

Both are used in the film to set the mood of a scene, and neither is heard by the characters. "Green Is the Colour" is one of the album's acoustic folk ballads, with Nick Mason's wife Lindy playing penny whistle. Live, the band often played it as a medley with "Careful with That Axe, Eugene" in a suite called "The Journey", and kept it in the set for around two years.

The Nile Song

The album's heaviest track, a hard rock song that sounds nothing like the rest of the record. It was released as a single in France, Japan and New Zealand, and appeared with "Cirrus Minor" on the 1971 compilation Relics.

Crying Song and Cymbaline

In the film, both play on the transistor radio of the lead actress, Mimsy Farmer, in her apartment. "Cymbaline" is a jab at the music business, with a line about a manager and agent both busy on the phone. The album version differs from the one in the film, though Gilmour sings lead on both, even though Waters is sometimes wrongly credited for the film version. Live, it was renamed "Nightmare": the band walked off partway through while a tape of quadraphonic sound effects, including opening doors and footsteps around the hall, played to the audience. It stayed in the repertoire until the end of 1971.

Up the Khyber

The only time Mason and Wright are credited as the sole writers of a Pink Floyd piece. It is a studio take of a keyboard and drums duet that was a regular part of live versions of "Interstellar Overdrive" at the time.

Ibiza Bar

As its name suggests, it plays in the background of a bar scene in the film. It is the other hard rock track on the album, alongside "The Nile Song".

Quicksilver and Main Theme

Two of the album's experimental instrumentals. "Quicksilver" was played live in 1969 under the title "Sleeping", as part of a show called "The Man". "Main Theme" was only played briefly on stage, in 1970.

Left off the album

Three pieces can be heard in the film but not on the record: "Seabirds", "Hollywood" and "Theme (Beat)", a faster version of "Main Theme". The last two, plus an alternative take of "More Blues", were released in 2016 on the box set The Early Years 1965–1972.
