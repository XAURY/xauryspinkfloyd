More

Gear: More

The album was made while the band were still touring their previous record, so Gilmour's setup is similar, with one big change later in 1969: he moved from Selmer to Hiwatt amps.

Guitars

Gilmour played a white Fender Stratocaster, plus acoustic guitars for the quieter songs, and nylon-string classical guitar on a few tracks. On stage from spring 1969 he also used a natural-finish Telecaster. The band didn't tour this album, but spent much of 1969 playing the Man and the Journey show, which included songs from it.

Amplifiers

No reliable source says which amps were used in the studio. Since the sessions came before his Hiwatts, he probably used his Selmer amps or ones already in the studio. The first Hiwatts only went on sale in spring 1969, and Gilmour was one of the early users, switching to them around the summer.

Effects

A Dallas Arbiter Fuzz Face, a wah-wah (a Vox, later a Cry Baby) and a Binson Echorec delay.

The rest of the band

Richard Wright: the gear list on Richard Wright's official website (compiled by the fan 'aZiMuTh', and not verified in detail) names a Farfisa Combo Compact-Duo organ, a Hammond M-102 'Spinet' organ, an acoustic piano and a vibraphone for this album. Wikipedia's credits agree.

Roger Waters played bass and made the tape effects. Nick Mason played drums, plus bongos and other percussion. His wife Lindy played penny whistle on two tracks.

We haven't found a reliable source for the exact model of Waters's bass or Mason's drum kit, so they are left out for now.
