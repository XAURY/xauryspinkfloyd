More

Gear: More

The album was recorded while Pink Floyd were still touring A Saucerful of Secrets, so Gilmour's setup is much the same, with a big change coming partway through 1969: the Selmer amps were swapped for Hiwatts.

Guitars

On the album

  • Fender Stratocaster, 1966–67, whiteAll stock, with a white ash body, white pickguard and a rosewood 4-bolt neck with a large headstock.
  • Levin acoustic steel-string guitar
  • Acoustic nylon-string guitarGilmour is also credited with classical guitar on a few tracks.

Live in 1969

  • Fender Stratocaster, whiteThe same guitar as in the studio.
  • Fender Telecaster, natural brownMaple neck and white pickguard, with an uncovered neck pickup that may be a replaced or modified one. First seen on 14 April 1969, and used mainly for performing Atom Heart Mother in 1970–71.
  • Levin acoustic steel-string guitar

Amplifiers

On the album

No footage or reliable source shows which amps Gilmour used for the sessions. They were recorded before he started using Hiwatts, so he probably used the Selmer Stereomaster 100-watt heads and cabinets, or amps already in the studio.

Live in 1969

  • Selmer Stereomaster 100-watt headWith a probable Selmer 1x18 Goliath cabinet. Used until the summer.
  • Two Hiwatt DR103 All Purpose 100-watt headsThe start of a long relationship with Hiwatt. They replaced the Selmers around July or August, so they were not used on the album.
  • Two WEM Super Starfinder 200 cabinetsProbably loaded with 4x12 Fane Crescendo speakers with metal dust caps.

The first Hiwatts were only on sale from around March or April 1969, which puts Gilmour among the very first users, along with The Who and Jethro Tull.

Effects

  • Dallas Arbiter Fuzz FaceThe germanium-transistor version.
  • Vox wah-wah and Cry Baby wah-wahGilmour swapped between the two in the early 1970s until settling on the Cry Baby in 1977. Footage from 1968 shows a Vox, and from the Royal Festival Hall in April 1969 a Cry Baby.
  • Binson Echorec II

Song by song

  • Cirrus MinorAcoustic steel-string guitar.
  • The Nile SongStratocaster, bridge pickup. Rhythm and solo through the amp or the Fuzz Face.
  • Crying SongNylon-string acoustic for the rhythm, and Stratocaster for the solo and slide through the amp or the Fuzz Face, with two octave-apart guitars. Live, he used the neck pickup with a little echo.
  • Main ThemeStratocaster, bridge pickup. Slide with a clean sound and echo.
  • Ibiza BarStratocaster, bridge pickup. Two rhythm guitars and the solo, all through the Fuzz Face.
  • More BluesStratocaster, bridge pickup. Solo through the amp or the Fuzz Face.

Miscellaneous equipment

The band never toured the album. Instead they spent much of 1969 playing a suite called The Man and the Journey, which mixed songs from More with material from A Saucerful of Secrets, Ummagumma and new pieces. It premiered at the Royal Festival Hall on 14 April 1969.

The rest of the band

Richard Wright: the gear list on Richard Wright's official website (compiled by the fan 'aZiMuTh', and not verified in detail) names a Farfisa Combo Compact-Duo organ, a Hammond M-102 'Spinet' organ, an acoustic piano and a vibraphone for this album. Wikipedia's credits agree, and give the Hammond to "Cirrus Minor".

Roger Waters played bass and made the tape effects. Nick Mason played drums, plus bongos and other percussion. His wife Lindy played penny whistle on two tracks.

We haven't found a reliable source for the exact model of Waters's bass or Mason's drum kit, so they are left out for now.
