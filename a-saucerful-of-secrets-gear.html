A Saucerful of Secrets

Gear: A Saucerful of Secrets

David Gilmour's first album with the band. In his first year he kept using much of Syd Barrett's old equipment, Telecasters and Selmer amps included.

Guitars

Gilmour mainly played a Fender Telecaster from the early 1960s, a 21st-birthday present from his parents. In early 1968 he also borrowed Syd Barrett's Telecaster as a spare. After his own went missing on the band's first US trip in July 1968, he switched to a white Fender Stratocaster given to him by the band.

Barrett himself still plays on a few tracks, such as slide guitar on "Remember a Day" and the guitar on "Jugband Blues".

Amplifiers

Like Barrett, Gilmour used Selmer amplifiers. Sources disagree on the exact model for the album, as both the Treble 'n' Bass and the Stereomaster appear around this time. Turned up loud, they gave his guitar a fuzzy edge.

Effects

A Dallas Arbiter Fuzz Face, a wah-wah pedal and a Binson Echorec delay, the same echo unit Barrett had used. Much of the album's overdrive came from turning up the amps, not from pedals.

The rest of the band

Richard Wright: the gear list on Richard Wright's official website (compiled by the fan 'aZiMuTh', and not verified in detail) names a Farfisa Combo Compact-Duo organ, a Hammond M-102 'Spinet' organ, an acoustic piano, a Mellotron M400 Mark II and a vibraphone for this period. Wikipedia's credits add a celesta, a xylophone, a tin whistle and a kazoo on some tracks.

Roger Waters played bass on every track, with percussion on two. Nick Mason played drums and percussion.

We haven't found a reliable source for the exact model of Waters's bass or Mason's drum kit, so they are left out for now.
