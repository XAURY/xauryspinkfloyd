A Saucerful of Secrets

Gear: A Saucerful of Secrets

David Gilmour's first album with the band. In his first year he kept using much of Syd Barrett's old equipment, Telecasters and Selmer amps included, and in early 1968 he even borrowed Syd's own guitar and amp as spares.

Guitars

On the album

  • Fender Telecaster, early 1960sAll stock, with a white ash body, white pickguard and rosewood neck. This was Gilmour's own guitar, a 21st-birthday present from his parents.
  • A second, identical TelecasterSyd Barrett's, borrowed by Gilmour as a spare in early 1968.
  • Levin acoustic steel-string guitarUsed for the acoustic parts.

Barrett himself plays the guitar parts on "Remember a Day", the studio version of "Set the Controls for the Heart of the Sun" and "Jugband Blues".

Live, 1968 to spring 1969

  • The same white Telecaster, plus Barrett's spareGilmour lost his own Telecaster during the band's US visit in July 1968.
  • Fender Stratocaster, 1966–67, whiteA gift from the band just before that US tour, with a rosewood neck and a large headstock. It replaced the lost Telecaster.
  • Fender Telecaster, natural brownMaple neck and white pickguard, with an uncovered neck pickup that may be a replaced or modified one. First seen at the Royal Festival Hall on 14 April 1969.
  • Levin acoustic steel-string guitar

Amplifiers

  • Selmer amps with matching cabinetsSources disagree on the exact model used for the album. He may have used a 50-watt Treble 'n' Bass MkII, as Barrett did, but a Selmer Stereomaster is seen in the studio footage.
  • Selmer Stereomaster 100-watt headSeen in the studio and the one he seems to have preferred live.
  • Selmer cabinetsA 2x12 All Purpose and probably a 1x18 Goliath, the latter seen from autumn 1968.

Both Selmer models sound a little like an early Marshall, with less midrange emphasis and a fuzz-like edge when turned up.

Effects

  • Dallas Arbiter Fuzz FaceThe germanium-transistor version.
  • Vox wah-wah
  • Binson Echorec IIThe echo unit Barrett had used before him. Gilmour kept using Binson echoes for years.

Much of the overdrive on the album came from turning the Selmers up, not from pedals, and Gilmour rolled his guitar's volume down for rhythm parts. A rough guide for the Fuzz Face is volume at about 70% and fuzz fully up, and the Binson at around 300 ms in repeat mode. These settings are estimates from photos and interviews, so use them as a starting point only.

Song by song

  • Let There Be More LightTelecaster, bridge pickup. Clean for the melody, and Selmer amp or Fuzz Face for the rhythm and lead.
  • Corporal CleggTelecaster, bridge pickup. Three guitars, through the Selmer or the Fuzz Face, with a wah-wah on the chorus rhythm.
  • A Saucerful of SecretsTelecaster, bridge pickup. Slide effects with the Selmer or Fuzz Face and echo. Live, a slide is rubbed over the frets to make a violin-like sound.
  • See-SawTelecaster with wah-wah, and acoustic guitar for the rhythm.

Miscellaneous equipment

In 1968 the band used a lot of in-house and borrowed gear. They often filmed TV shows in France during the day, then flew back to play shows in the UK the same night. They also did not take their own PA system to the US in July 1968, and used speakers supplied by the venues or the headlining bands.

The rest of the band

Richard Wright: the gear list on Richard Wright's official website (compiled by the fan 'aZiMuTh', and not verified in detail) names a Farfisa Combo Compact-Duo organ, a Hammond M-102 'Spinet' organ, an acoustic piano, a Mellotron M400 Mark II and a vibraphone for this period.

Wikipedia's credits for the album add a celesta, a xylophone, a tin whistle and a kazoo, played by Wright on some tracks. These come from Wikipedia and are not on that list.

Roger Waters played bass on every track, with percussion on "Set the Controls for the Heart of the Sun" and the title track. Nick Mason played drums and percussion, and a kazoo on "Jugband Blues".

We haven't found a reliable source for the exact model of Waters's bass or Mason's drum kit, so they are left out for now.
