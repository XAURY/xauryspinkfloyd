The story

Pink Floyd began as a student band in London in 1965 and became one of the best-selling groups in rock. Along the way they changed sound, lineup and leader more than once.

1965–1968

Syd Barrett and the London underground

The band formed around architecture students Roger Waters, Richard Wright and Nick Mason, with Barrett, a friend of Waters from Cambridge, as guitarist, singer and main songwriter. The name came from two blues musicians, Pink Anderson and Floyd Council, and was Barrett's idea.

They became the house band of London's underground scene, known for long improvised pieces and light shows. Two singles, "Arnold Layne" and "See Emily Play", led to their debut album in 1967. As Barrett's health and behavior became unreliable, his school friend David Gilmour joined in early 1968 on guitar. Barrett left a few months later.

The Piper at the Gates of Dawn

1968–1972

Finding a new sound

Without Barrett, the band shared the songwriting and turned to long, atmospheric pieces, film soundtracks and studio experiments. Gilmour's guitar became a central voice. Meddle, with the side-long piece "Echoes", set the direction for what came next.

Albums

A Saucerful of Secrets More Ummagumma Atom Heart Mother Meddle Obscured by Clouds

1973–1977

The peak years

The Dark Side of the Moon (1973) turned the band into a worldwide success and is one of the best-selling albums ever made. Wish You Were Here was partly a tribute to Barrett, and Animals was a harder, more cynical record. From this period Waters wrote most of the lyrics and Gilmour's guitar playing defined the sound.

The Dark Side of the Moon Wish You Were Here Animals

1979–1983

The Wall and the split in the band

The Wall was a double album and stage show built around Waters's story. Tensions grew during the recording, and Wright left the band, though he played on the tour as a paid musician. The Final Cut followed in 1983 and was largely Waters's project.

Albums

The Wall The Final Cut

1985–1995

Gilmour takes the lead

Waters left in 1985, and a legal dispute over who could use the Pink Floyd name followed. Gilmour and Mason kept the band going, with Wright returning, first as a session player and then as a full member. They released two studio albums and toured stadiums around the world, and both tours produced live albums.

Albums

A Momentary Lapse of Reason Delicate Sound of Thunder The Division Bell PULSE

2005–2014

Reunion and farewell

In 2005 Waters, Gilmour, Wright and Mason played together at the Live 8 concert in London, their first time on stage together in over twenty years. Barrett died in 2006 and Wright in 2008. The band's last album, The Endless River (2014), was built from unused recordings made during The Division Bell sessions and is a tribute to Wright.

The members

David Gilmour
Guitar and vocals. Joined in 1968 and led the band after 1985.
Roger Waters
Bass and vocals. Founding member and main lyricist from the 1970s. Left in 1985.
Richard Wright
Keyboards and vocals. Founding member, 1943–2008. Left after The Wall and returned in 1987.
Nick Mason
Drums. Founding member, and the only one who played on every album.
Syd Barrett
Guitar, vocals and songwriting. Founding member, 1946–2006. Left in 1968.
